Northwind Logistics — Finance, Travel and Procurement Policy (FY2026)

Purpose and scope
This policy sets out how Northwind Logistics pays vendors, approves purchases, reimburses employee expenses and manages company equipment. It applies to all permanent employees, contract staff and interns in India and in overseas branch offices. Where a local law sets a stricter rule, the local law applies. Questions about this policy should be sent to the Finance Controller's office. The policy is reviewed every April and changes take effect from the first day of the financial year.

1. Vendor payment terms
Standard vendor payment terms are net 45 days from the invoice date. Strategic suppliers listed in Annex B may be paid on net 30 terms after approval from the Finance Controller. Freight carriers paid per shipment are settled weekly, every Friday, for all deliveries confirmed by Wednesday of the same week. Any early-payment discount offered by a vendor must be at least 2 percent to be taken. Vendors must quote the purchase order number on every invoice; invoices without a purchase order number are returned to the vendor unpaid.

2. Purchase approval limits
Purchase orders up to 2,50,000 rupees may be approved by a department head. Orders above 2,50,000 rupees and up to 25,00,000 rupees require approval from the Finance Controller. Orders above 25,00,000 rupees require approval from the Chief Financial Officer. Splitting a single purchase into smaller orders to stay under an approval limit is a disciplinary offence. Capital purchases of vehicles and warehouse equipment follow the same limits but must also be recorded in the fixed asset register within 15 days of delivery.

3. Domestic travel
Domestic flights must be booked in economy class through the company travel desk at least 7 days before departure where possible. Train travel in AC two-tier is permitted for journeys under 600 kilometres. Hotel stays are capped at 9,000 rupees per night in metro cities and 6,000 rupees per night in tier-2 cities. Elsewhere in India the cap is 4,500 rupees per night. The domestic daily meal allowance is 1,500 rupees and covers breakfast, lunch and dinner; meals provided by a client or at a conference are deducted from the allowance.

4. International travel
International flights longer than 8 hours may be booked in premium economy; shorter international flights must be booked in economy. Hotel stays abroad are capped at 180 US dollars per night, or 250 US dollars per night in London, New York, Singapore and Tokyo. The international daily meal allowance is 60 US dollars. Travel insurance is arranged by the travel desk for every international trip and must not be purchased separately. Employees travelling abroad for more than 30 days in a financial year must inform the tax team before departure.

5. Local conveyance
Local taxis and app-based cabs are reimbursed at actual cost for client visits. Use of a personal car for business travel is reimbursed at 12 rupees per kilometre and a personal two-wheeler at 5 rupees per kilometre. Daily commuting between home and the employee's usual office is not reimbursed. Parking and toll charges are reimbursed with a receipt.

6. Expense submission
Employees must submit expense claims within 30 days of incurring the expense. Claims submitted after 60 days will be rejected unless approved by the Finance Controller. Every claim above 2,000 rupees requires an itemised receipt; claims of 2,000 rupees or less may be submitted with a self-declaration. Claims are reviewed by the employee's manager and then by the finance team, and approved claims are paid with the next monthly salary.

7. Corporate cards
Corporate cards are issued to employees who travel at least twice a month. The monthly card limit is 1,00,000 rupees for managers and 50,000 rupees for other staff. Cash withdrawals on a corporate card are not permitted. Personal spending on a corporate card must be repaid within 7 days and repeated personal use leads to the card being withdrawn.

8. Late payments and escalation
Overdue vendor invoices older than 90 days are escalated to the Accounts Payable Manager every Monday. Invoices older than 120 days are escalated to the Finance Controller. Interest on late payments, where contractually owed, is charged to the cost centre that delayed approval. Disputed invoices are excluded from the overdue report while the dispute is open.

9. Company equipment
Laptops are replaced on a 4-year refresh cycle, or earlier if a repair would cost more than half the price of a new device. Mobile phones are replaced every 3 years. Software subscriptions costing more than 50,000 rupees per year must be approved by the IT Security team before purchase, in addition to the normal purchase approval limits. Lost or stolen devices must be reported to IT within 24 hours.

10. Gifts and hospitality
Employees may accept gifts from vendors worth up to 2,500 rupees per vendor per year. Gifts above that value must be declared to the compliance team and may have to be returned. Cash gifts and gift cards may never be accepted. Hospitality offered by a vendor during an active tender must be declined.
